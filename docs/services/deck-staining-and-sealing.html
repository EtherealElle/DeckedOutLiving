Deck Staining & Sealing

Sealing is the cheapest thing you can do to a wooden deck and the thing most likely to be skipped. Left bare, pine in Georgia greys, checks and starts holding water.

The job is mostly preparation

Anyone can roll stain onto a deck. Whether it lasts two seasons or five comes down to what happened before the can was opened.

  1. Clean. Strip off the grey surface layer, mildew and ground-in dirt. Pressure washing helps but over-doing it tears up soft grain, so it is worth doing carefully.
  2. Brighten. Cleaners leave wood chemically darkened. A brightener returns the pH and opens the grain so the stain can actually soak in.
  3. Dry. The step people skip. Wood needs to be genuinely dry through, not just dry to the touch, or the finish will not penetrate and will peel.
  4. Apply. Worked into the boards and the end grain, with railings, stairs and fascia included rather than just the flat surface.

Choosing a finish

FinishLooks likeTrade-off
Clear sealerNatural wood, barely changedLeast UV protection, so it greys soonest
Semi-transparentWood grain still visible, tintedGood balance; the usual choice for pine
Semi-solidGrain mostly hidden, colour strongLonger lasting, less wood character
SolidLike paintLongest colour life, but it can peel rather than fade, which makes recoating harder later

Timing it for Georgia

Two things get in the way here. Spring pollen will settle into wet stain and stay there, so late March through April is awkward. Summer afternoon storms give you a narrow dry window, and a surprise shower on fresh finish means doing it again. Late spring after the pollen drops, or early autumn, tends to give the most reliable conditions.

A brand-new deck needs to wait

Fresh pressure-treated pine holds a lot of moisture from the treatment process and will not accept a finish properly until it has dried out. That is usually a matter of months, not weeks. A simple test: sprinkle water on the boards — if it beads up, the wood is not ready. If it soaks in, it is.

Common questions

How often should a deck be re-sealed?

For pressure-treated pine in this climate, every two to three years is a reasonable expectation, sooner for surfaces in full sun all day. Horizontal boards wear faster than railings.

Do composite decks need sealing?

No. Composite is washed, not sealed. If someone offers to seal a composite deck, that is a reason to ask more questions.

Can you stain a deck that has never been treated?

Yes, and it is worth doing. It needs a full clean and brighten first, and older grey wood usually takes more product than people expect.

Will staining hide damaged boards?

No, and it should not. Stain makes a sound deck look good; it makes a soft board look like a soft board with colour on it. We would replace those first.
