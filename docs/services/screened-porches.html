Screened Porches

In middle Georgia the thing that stops people using a deck is rarely the weather. It is the mosquitoes.

Converting a deck, or building new

If you already have a sound deck in the right place, screening it in is often the cheaper route to the space you actually want. The deciding factors are whether the framing can carry a roof, whether the posts land where they need to, and the height and pitch you can get without fighting the existing roofline.

Where the deck is undersized, low, or in the wrong spot, building purpose-made is usually the better answer — a porch designed as a porch from the framing up avoids a lot of compromises.

Things worth deciding early

  • Ceiling. An open rafter ceiling feels bigger; a finished one feels like part of the house and hides wiring.
  • Floor. Standard decking works, but a covered porch stays wetter for longer after rain, so gapping and drainage still matter.
  • Door swing. Where the screen door lands decides your furniture layout more than people expect.
  • Fans and lighting. Much easier before the ceiling goes up.
  • Future glass. If you might enclose it properly one day, framing for that now is far cheaper than retrofitting later.

The season it buys you

A screened porch adds usable weeks at both ends of the year — spring evenings before the bugs arrive in force, and autumn nights when the deck is otherwise too cool to sit out comfortably. That is the practical argument for it over an open deck.

Common questions

Can my existing deck be screened in?

Often, yes. It depends on whether the framing can take a roof and where the support posts land. That is the first thing we look at when we come out.

Is a screened porch warmer than a deck?

Slightly, because it cuts wind and holds a little heat under the roof, but it is not a heated room. If you want year-round use, you are looking at glass rather than screen.

How hard is it to replace a torn screen?

For most systems it is straightforward and panel-by-panel, which is one reason it is worth using a proper screen system rather than stapling mesh to the framing.

Can it be built to convert to a sunroom later?

Yes, if it is planned that way. Framing, footings and roof structure for a future enclosure are much cheaper to build in now than to add later.
